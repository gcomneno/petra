# AIP-4 — Minimal PETRA algebra

Status: **research formalization** for issue #279 under the complete-theory programme #276.

This note studies whether the intrinsic PETRA rewrite algebra is generated by two elementary structural operations. It does not assume the answer in advance and does not change the normative SPEC/runtime/API/CLI/serialization/Resolver behavior.

The carrier basis is the canonical research carrier from #277 / PR #278: PETRA forms are isomorphism classes of finite rooted non-plane tree realizations, with primitive direct parent-child incidence, intrinsic multiplicity, no intrinsic sibling order, and no persistent occurrence identity.

### Carrier assumption used by AIP-4

All lemmas and theorems below use the explicit concrete-realization conditions of `canonical-carrier.md`, including that the distinguished root has no immediate parent, every non-root occurrence has exactly one immediate parent, and every occurrence is reachable from the root. Acyclicity is a theorem derived from those conditions rather than an independent assumption.

## 1. Pointed realizations and unpointed forms

### Definition 1 — Pointed PETRA realization

A **pointed realization** is a pair `(R,u)` where `R=(V,r,->)` is a concrete PETRA realization and `u in V` is a chosen occurrence.

Pointing is operation-local targeting information. It is not persistent PETRA ontology.

### Definition 2 — Pointed incidence

A **pointed child incidence** is a triple `(R,u,v)` with `u -> v` in `R`.

The child occurrence `v` identifies one concrete incidence even when several children support isomorphic subforms.

### Remark 1 — Why pointing is required

A bare PETRA form is an isomorphism class and may have non-trivial automorphisms. Therefore a target occurrence is not, in general, canonically determined by the unpointed form alone.

The elementary operations below are single-valued on pointed realizations. After forgetting the point and quotienting by realization isomorphism, they induce binary relations on PETRA forms rather than globally single-valued functions.

## 2. Candidate B: elementary node editing

Candidate B is the genuinely local hypothesis.

### Definition 3 — Elementary ADD

For a pointed realization `(R,u)`, define `ADD_NODE(R,u)` by adjoining one fresh occurrence `z` and one new direct incidence

```text
u -> z
```

with `z` having no children. All pre-existing vertices and incidences are unchanged.

Thus exactly one new zero-child occurrence is attached as a child of `u`.

### Definition 4 — Elementary REMOVE

For a pointed child incidence `(R,u,v)`, `REMOVE_LEAF(R,u,v)` is defined iff `v` has no children.

When defined, remove the incidence `u -> v` and remove the occurrence `v`. All other vertices and incidences are unchanged.

The root occurrence is never removed because it has no parent incidence.

### Lemma 1 — Closure of elementary ADD

`ADD_NODE` maps every pointed PETRA realization to a PETRA realization.

#### Proof

The result remains finite and non-empty. The old root remains distinguished and still has no parent because the new incidence is outgoing from an existing occurrence to the fresh leaf. The new occurrence `z` has exactly one parent, namely `u`; every old non-root occurrence keeps its unique parent. Every old occurrence remains reachable from the root, and `z` is reachable by the old root-to-`u` path followed by `u -> z`. No existing incidence is changed. Hence the defining rooted-tree conditions remain satisfied. QED.

### Lemma 2 — Closure of elementary REMOVE

Whenever `REMOVE_LEAF(R,u,v)` is defined, its result is again a PETRA realization.

#### Proof

Only a non-root zero-child occurrence `v` and its unique incoming incidence are removed. The root is unchanged and remains parentless. No remaining occurrence loses its parent, because `v` has no descendants. Every remaining occurrence remains reachable from the root, and finiteness is preserved. QED.

### Definition 5 — Induced ADD relation on PETRA forms

For PETRA forms `P,Q`, write

```text
P --ADD--> Q
```

iff there exists a realization `R` of `P` and an occurrence `u` of `R` such that forgetting occurrence labels after `ADD_NODE(R,u)` yields a realization of `Q`.

### Definition 6 — Induced REMOVE relation on PETRA forms

Write

```text
P --REMOVE--> Q
```

iff there exists a realization `R` of `P` and a zero-child non-root occurrence `v` with parent `u` such that forgetting occurrence labels after `REMOVE_LEAF(R,u,v)` yields a realization of `Q`.

### Lemma 3 — Isomorphism invariance

The induced relations `--ADD-->` and `--REMOVE-->` are well-defined on PETRA forms: changing the chosen concrete representative of an isomorphism class does not change the set of resulting PETRA forms.

#### Proof

Let `f:R≅S` be a root-preserving realization isomorphism. Choosing `u` in `R` corresponds to choosing `f(u)` in `S`. Extending `f` by mapping the fresh leaf added at `u` to the fresh leaf added at `f(u)` gives an isomorphism between the two ADD results.

For REMOVE, an incidence `u->v` with `v` zero-child corresponds under `f` to `f(u)->f(v)`, and deleting the corresponding leaf occurrences restricts `f` to an isomorphism of the two REMOVE results. QED.

## 3. Inverse structure

### Theorem 1 — ADD and REMOVE are converse relations

For PETRA forms `P,Q`,

```text
P --ADD--> Q    iff    Q --REMOVE--> P.
```

#### Proof

If `P --ADD--> Q`, choose a witnessing realization and target occurrence. The newly added occurrence is zero-child and has exactly one incoming incidence. Removing exactly that leaf incidence reconstructs the original realization, hence `Q --REMOVE--> P`.

Conversely, if `Q --REMOVE--> P`, restore the removed zero-child occurrence at its former parent. This is exactly an elementary ADD and reconstructs a realization of `Q`. QED.

### Corollary 1 — Pointed partial inverse

On pointed realizations, `REMOVE_LEAF` is a partial inverse of `ADD_NODE` when applied to the newly created incidence, and every valid `REMOVE_LEAF` step is reversed by one `ADD_NODE` step at the same parent occurrence.

## 4. Size grading

### Theorem 2 — Unit size change

If

```text
P --ADD--> Q
```

then

```text
size(Q) = size(P) + 1.
```

If

```text
P --REMOVE--> Q
```

then

```text
size(Q) = size(P) - 1.
```

#### Proof

Elementary ADD introduces exactly one occurrence and elementary REMOVE deletes exactly one occurrence. QED.

### Corollary 2 — No non-trivial ADD cycle or REMOVE cycle

A non-empty sequence consisting only of ADD steps cannot return to its starting PETRA form, and similarly for REMOVE-only sequences.

#### Proof

Size changes strictly monotonically by one at every step. QED.

## 5. Generation from the zero-child form

Let

```text
Z = Node(empty).
```

### Theorem 3 — Construction theorem

Every finite PETRA form `P` can be obtained from `Z` by a finite sequence of elementary ADD steps.

More precisely, every `P` of size `n` can be constructed using exactly `n-1` ADD steps.

#### Proof

Choose any concrete realization `R=(V,r,->)` of `P`. Since `R` is a finite rooted tree, order its non-root occurrences so that every parent occurs before its children; for example, use non-decreasing level.

Start with the one-occurrence realization of `Z`, corresponding to `r`. Process the remaining occurrences in that order. When processing occurrence `v`, its parent has already been constructed. Apply one elementary ADD at that parent to create a fresh zero-child occurrence corresponding to `v`.

Later ADD steps at `v` create exactly its children. Because sibling order is irrelevant, no ordering choice among siblings affects the resulting PETRA form. After all `|V|-1` non-root occurrences have been added, the constructed realization is root-preserving isomorphic to `R`. Therefore its PETRA form is `P`.

Exactly one new occurrence is created per ADD step, so `n-1` steps are necessary and sufficient by Theorem 2. QED.

### Theorem 4 — Reduction theorem

Every finite PETRA form `P` can be reduced to `Z` by a finite sequence of elementary REMOVE steps.

More precisely, every `P` of size `n` reduces to `Z` using exactly `n-1` REMOVE steps.

#### Proof

Every finite rooted tree with more than one occurrence has at least one non-root leaf occurrence. Remove any such leaf. By Lemma 2 the result remains a PETRA realization and has size one less.

Repeat. Finiteness guarantees termination at size one, whose unique PETRA form is `Z`. Exactly `n-1` removals occur by Theorem 2. QED.

### Corollary 3 — Connectivity theorem

For any finite PETRA forms `P` and `Q`, there exists a finite sequence of elementary ADD/REMOVE steps transforming `P` into `Q`.

#### Proof

Reduce `P` to `Z` by Theorem 4, then construct `Q` from `Z` by Theorem 3. QED.

### Corollary 4 — Coarse path-length bound

There exists an ADD/REMOVE path from `P` to `Q` of length

```text
size(P) + size(Q) - 2.
```

This is an upper bound obtained through `Z`; it is not claimed to be shortest.

## 6. Minimality of the two directions

### Theorem 5 — ADD alone is not transformation-complete

ADD alone cannot generate the full transformation relation on PETRA forms.

#### Proof

By Theorem 2 every ADD strictly increases size. Therefore no larger form can be transformed into a smaller form using only ADD. QED.

### Theorem 6 — REMOVE alone is not transformation-complete

REMOVE alone cannot generate the full transformation relation on PETRA forms.

#### Proof

By Theorem 2 every REMOVE strictly decreases size. Therefore no smaller form can be transformed into a larger form using only REMOVE. QED.

### Corollary 5 — Directional minimality

If the intended algebra must permit reachability between every ordered pair of finite PETRA forms, at least one size-increasing and at least one size-decreasing primitive direction are required.

Within Candidate B, both ADD and REMOVE are therefore necessary.

This does not yet prove uniqueness of the generator set among all possible algebraic signatures.

## 7. Candidate A: arbitrary subtree editing

### Definition 7 — Arbitrary-form ADD

`ADD_FORM(R,u,X)` attaches a realization of an arbitrary PETRA form `X` as one new child subtree of occurrence `u`.

### Definition 8 — Arbitrary-subtree REMOVE

`REMOVE_SUBTREE(R,u,v)` removes the incidence `u->v` and deletes the entire descendant subtree rooted at `v`.

### Proposition 1 — Candidate A is closed

Both Candidate-A operations preserve the PETRA carrier whenever the REMOVE target is non-root.

#### Proof

Attaching a finite rooted tree by one incidence preserves finiteness, connectedness, unique parenthood, and acyclicity. Removing a complete non-root descendant subtree leaves the root component finite and connected, with unchanged parenthood among remaining occurrences. QED.

### Proposition 2 — Candidate A subsumes Candidate B

Elementary `ADD_NODE` is `ADD_FORM` with `X=Z`. Elementary `REMOVE_LEAF` is `REMOVE_SUBTREE` restricted to a zero-child child subtree.

Therefore every Candidate-B edit sequence is also a Candidate-A edit sequence.

### Remark 2 — Expressiveness versus primitiveness

Candidate A is sufficient for global reachability, but that fact is weaker as a minimal-algebra result because one primitive step may create or delete an arbitrarily large recursive structure.

Candidate B is strictly more elementary with respect to node count: every primitive step changes size by exactly one.

This note therefore treats Candidate B as the stronger candidate for a minimal intrinsic generator set, while keeping the conclusion research-level until executable corroboration and external algebraic comparison are complete.

## 8. Candidate C: incidence-only editing

### Proposition 3 — Pure incidence deletion is not closed on PETRA

Deleting a parent-child incidence while retaining both endpoint occurrences generally leaves the PETRA carrier.

#### Proof

Deleting `u->v` disconnects the descendant subtree rooted at `v` from the distinguished root, while the detached occurrences remain present. The resulting structure violates root reachability. QED.

### Proposition 4 — Pure incidence insertion is not generally closed on PETRA

Adding an incidence between two already-present occurrences is not, in general, a PETRA operation.

#### Proof

If the proposed child already has a parent, adding another incoming incidence violates unique immediate parenthood. If the added incidence targets an ancestor, it creates a directed cycle. The rooted-tree carrier therefore does not admit unrestricted incidence-only insertion as a closed primitive operation. QED.

### Corollary 6 — Node creation/deletion is intrinsic to closed elementary editing

Within the PETRA carrier itself, a closed local edge edit cannot in general be separated from the corresponding creation or deletion of an endpoint occurrence. An incidence-only algebra would require a larger ambient category such as rooted forests or partially detached structures.

## 9. Automorphisms and target ambiguity

### Definition 9 — Unpointed successor set

For a PETRA form `P`, define

```text
ADD(P) = { Q | P --ADD--> Q },
REMOVE(P) = { Q | P --REMOVE--> Q }.
```

These are sets of PETRA forms, not necessarily singleton values.

### Proposition 5 — Bare-form ADD need not be single-valued

There exist PETRA forms `P` having two target occurrences such that ADD at those occurrences produces non-isomorphic results.

#### Proof

Take

```text
P = Node({ Z, Node({Z}) }).
```

Adding a new leaf to the root yields a root with child multiset

```text
{ Z, Z, Node({Z}) }.
```

Adding instead to the zero-child child occurrence yields

```text
Node({ Node({Z}), Node({Z}) }).
```

The two results have different root degrees and are therefore non-isomorphic. QED.

### Corollary 7 — Canonical positional targeting is not part of the abstract algebra

A deterministic bare-form function `ADD : P -> P` cannot be obtained merely from the carrier without adding a target-selection rule or additional structure.

The intrinsic abstract operation is therefore most naturally represented either as:

1. a pointed operation on `(P,u)` / pointed realizations; or
2. an unpointed binary relation on PETRA forms.

Positional ranks and ordered addresses may implement target selection in a representation, but they are not intrinsic carrier structure.

## 10. Historical four-operator classification

The previous bounded AIP probes used the following abstract effects:

```text
SPROUT: Node(M) -> Node(M multiset-union {Z})
SHED:   Node(M multiset-union {Z}) -> Node(M)
GRAFT:  Z -> Node({Z})
PRUNE:  Node({Z}) -> Z
```

Under those abstract effects:

### Proposition 6 — GRAFT and PRUNE are special cases

`GRAFT` is elementary ADD restricted to a zero-child target occurrence.

`PRUNE` is elementary REMOVE restricted to a leaf incidence whose parent has exactly one child and is itself the selected local form root.

### Proposition 7 — SPROUT and SHED are elementary ADD/REMOVE effects

At the carrier level, the stated SPROUT effect is elementary ADD at the selected parent occurrence, and the stated SHED effect is elementary REMOVE of one selected zero-child child incidence.

### Remark 3 — Historical targeting semantics remain representation-sensitive

The propositions classify only the abstract structural effects previously established by the AIP probes. They do not claim that every runtime precondition, address convention, witness shape, or historical operator distinction is intrinsic.

Thus the current evidence supports the research hypothesis that the four historical operator effects collapse structurally to two elementary directions once representation-specific targeting distinctions are erased.

## 11. Current AIP-4 conclusion

### Research conclusion

For the canonical finite rooted non-plane PETRA carrier, Candidate B supports a complete elementary reachability algebra generated by:

```text
ADD_NODE    — attach exactly one fresh zero-child node
REMOVE_LEAF — remove exactly one zero-child child node
```

with these properties proved in this note:

- closure;
- isomorphism invariance;
- converse/inverse relation;
- exact `+1/-1` size grading;
- construction of every finite PETRA form from `Z`;
- reduction of every finite PETRA form to `Z`;
- connectivity of the induced edit graph;
- necessity of both size directions for ordered-pair reachability;
- failure of unrestricted incidence-only editing to remain closed;
- necessity of pointed or relational semantics because bare-form target selection can be ambiguous.

The phrase “ADD / REMOVE generate the PETRA algebra” is therefore supported only with the precise elementary semantics above. Arbitrary-subtree ADD/REMOVE also generate reachability but are less elementary and do not provide the same minimality evidence.

## 12. What remains unproved or external

This note does **not** yet establish:

- uniqueness of `{ADD_NODE, REMOVE_LEAF}` among all possible generator signatures;
- a shortest-path formula between arbitrary PETRA forms;
- a complete presentation by generators and relations;
- confluence or normal-form claims for unrestricted bidirectional editing;
- novelty relative to established rooted-tree edit algebras;
- normative replacement of the historical four-operator API;
- AIP-5 interpretation semantics.

A bounded executable probe should next corroborate closure, inverse witnesses, connectivity on enumerated forms, target ambiguity, and the structural collapse of the historical four operator effects.
